Plot 67 · Plan 623 · Al-Ammariyah, Riyadh

A 3.6 hectare hillside, read from orbit.

This page puts one plot of land under every free scientific dataset that covers it: the survey's own coordinates, a 30 m terrain model, 25 years of NASA weather, 75 years of climate reanalysis, 11 years of Sentinel-2 passes and 40 years of Landsat. Everything is computed for this exact polygon.

Terrain & drainage التضاريس والسيول

The plot is not flat. It sits on the back slope of one of the long limestone ridges west of Wadi Hanifa, with its north-west edge running along the ridge crest.

✓
Water runs off this plot, not onto it.Flow routing on the terrain model shows the plot is at the top of its own small catchment: the most water any cell inside it collects is from about 2.6 ha. The ground is 16 to 71 m above the nearest drainage line (median about 37 m). The nearest real wadi channel (1.4 km² catchment) passes about 115 m south-east of corner 3.
!
Flattening it all would be a very big job.

The survey lists the western neighbour as "plot 68 + wadi". At 30 m resolution the terrain model sees the ridge crest there, not a channel, so that wadi is either a small gully below 30 m scale or the valley at the foot of the ridge. Worth checking on the ground.

Water underground المياه الجوفية

No satellite can see groundwater under a 3.6 ha plot. What the rock layers, the nearest measured wells and the land itself can tell you is below. The bottom line: the plot sits on a limestone ridge that sheds water, so any well would most likely be deep. The better bet for shallow water is the gravel in the wadi beds next to it.

The rock layers under the area
Central Tuwaiq Mountains, top to bottom. Hover a layer. Thicknesses from published sections; depths at the plot are not measured.
1
The surface rock is a poor aquifer.The ridges here belong to the Jurassic limestone belt of the Tuwaiq Mountains. The Tuwaiq Mountain Limestone is about 193 to 203 m thick and acts mostly as a barrier to water (an aquitard); the Hanifa limestone above it holds water only locally. Which of the two crops out on the plot itself needs the 1:250,000 Saudi Geological Survey sheet to settle.
2
The big aquifer is very deep.Riyadh's main deep aquifer, the Minjur sandstone, starts about 1,200 m below the city. The rock layers rise gently toward the west, so it should be somewhat shallower under the plot, but still hundreds of metres down. The Dhruma layer between them also carries water.
3
Nearby measured wells.In Wadi Nisah, south of Riyadh in the same kind of rock, the water level in 14 wells was 35 to 190 m below ground. Around Al-Jubailah in Wadi Hanifa, 40 to 55 km north-west of Riyadh (about 20 km north of the plot), farms pump from wells. In Wadi Hanifa itself the gravel aquifer was once reachable at about 10 m, before pumping drew it down.
4
The region is losing groundwater.GRACE gravity satellites measure groundwater across central and northern Saudi Arabia falling by about 4 mm of water a year. That is a regional figure (hundreds of kilometres), not a reading for this plot.
→
How to actually find out.A ground resistivity survey (ERT or TEM) along the wadi south-east of corner 3 and across the plot could show water-bearing gravel or fractured rock in a short field survey. Then a test borehole where the survey looks best. Drilling needs a permit from the Ministry of Environment, Water and Agriculture, which also keeps records of registered wells in the area.

Method & sources المنهجية والمصادر

How each number was made

  • Geometry: shoelace area and side lengths on the UTM 38N coordinates; geodesic area on WGS-84 with pyproj; UTM to lat/lon conversion compared with the sheet's own lat/lon.
  • Terrain: Copernicus GLO-30 DEM; priority-flood depression filling, D8 flow directions and flow accumulation over a 20 × 22 km window; height above nearest drainage (HAND) with a 0.5 km² channel threshold; slopes from 30 m central differences on a 5 m bilinear resample.
  • Weather: NASA POWER daily (2001 to 2025) and hourly (2020 to 2024); ERA5 via Open-Meteo (1950 to 2025). Reference ET from the FAO-56 Penman-Monteith equation using POWER Tmax, Tmin, dew point, wind at 2 m, solar radiation and surface pressure.
  • Solar: NOAA sun position; isotropic sky; albedo 0.3; SAPM open-rack cell temperature; −0.37 %/°C; 14 % system losses (incl. soiling) and a 96 % inverter; tracker = horizontal north-south axis, ±60°, no backtracking. Results are on the conservative side of published Riyadh yields.
  • Satellite: Sentinel-2 L2A band means over the polygon from the Planetary Computer statistics API, with the −1000 offset applied to scenes after 25 Jan 2022. Land cover: Impact Observatory annual v02.
  • Earthworks: balanced cut and fill to one level over the whole plot from the resampled DEM. A 30 m model smooths ridges, so treat it as an order of magnitude.
